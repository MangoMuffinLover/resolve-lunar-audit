03Method & geometryProtocols, frames and analysis definitions+

Method & assumptions

Hold the question still. Change one modeling choice.

  1. Record the question and controls. Retain the protocols, site, observer convention, visibility criterion, date domain and research benchmarks before interpreting outcomes.
  2. Use traceable terrain representations. Compare native NASA grids within one common 20 × 20 km local footprint. Keep the 80 m / 240 m exterior continuation fixed. Pixel spacing does not mean uniform pole-wide coverage.
  3. Construct the geometric horizon. Use bilinear DEM interpolation, spherical curvature and sampled horizon rays. Geometric visibility requires a body’s center above the represented skyline; it is not a power or radio-link prediction.
  4. Refine event boundaries. Keep reporting-domain clipping separate from complete transition-bounded episodes intersecting that domain. Preserve the late solar episode separately from the longest solar episode.
  5. Test numerics and retain counterexamples. Compare spatial, temporal and range refinements before interpreting model effects. Preserve the negative result and unresolved historical discrepancy.

06Limits & unresolved questionsWhat the evidence cannot establish+

Interpretation limits

A precise number is not necessarily a stable decision.

  • Representation response: different products and observer conventions can change a modeled answer. No product is designated terrain truth; finer pixel spacing does not certify accuracy.
  • Scoped stability: dGR203’s tested decision can remain unchanged while durations and Earth-only results change. This does not establish stability at dGR101 or every lunar site.
  • Censoring: the roughly 5.4-day registered nominal value is clipped. It must not become a claim of a physical 6.5-day mission failure; its complete R-01C simultaneous episode is about 7.40 days.
  • Limited uncertainty coverage: the first-five local-clone pilot is not a calibrated distribution. Exterior terrain, shared biases and all systematic errors are not sampled.
  • Geometry only: geometric center visibility omits mission power storage, radiance, eclipsing-source effects and communications link budgets. Finite-disc criteria in R-01B are changed observables with their own limits.
  • Historical replication: 12-hour increment counting explains part of the seven-versus-8.47-day gap; a one-day residual and inconsistent archived raster semantics remain unresolved. A diagnostic that produces seven days is not proof of the original authors’ implementation.
  • Product verification: automated checks do not establish terrain accuracy, accessibility certification or human comprehension. The uncoached twenty-second judge test and dedicated screen-reader review remain outstanding.
