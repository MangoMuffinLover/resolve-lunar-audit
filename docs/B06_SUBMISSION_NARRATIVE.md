# Robustness Evaluation of South-Polar Observation and Lunar Visibility Estimates (RESOLVE)

working submission narrative

These are reusable drafts grounded in the delivered evidence. They are not a submitted entry, confirmation of current competition requirements, or a claim that an award criterion has been met.

## Project description

RESOLVE audits whether a modeled lunar visibility window remains stable across defensible terrain representations and analysis choices. It connects site/date exploration to a result audit, measured factor effects, physical horizon geometry and traceable scientific evidence.

At dGR101, two native NASA terrain representations give 7.40 and 12.92 complete days of simultaneous geometric Sun/Earth visibility at a common absolute observer. The 5.53-day contrast survives replacing synthetic averaging with native products. RESOLVE exposes the modeled horizon mechanism while retaining observer-convention effects, reporting cutoffs and the limits of numerical validation.

The research also preserves a negative result: the tested first-five local-clone pilot at dGR203 did not change the specified research classification. That finding redirected the investigation rather than being discarded.

RESOLVE provides a bounded three-site navigator, a modeling-factor audit, a physical explanation and a source review layer. It reports measured model responses without assigning terrain-truth rankings, fabricated confidence scores or mission certification.

## Problem and resulting behavior

A precise prediction can conceal a sensitive modeling choice. Terrain representation, observer height, visibility semantics, numerical sampling and reporting domains can answer different questions or produce different modeled durations. A duration alone does not reveal these dependencies.

RESOLVE lets a reviewer start from a result, audit an explicitly matched comparison, see which factor changed, inspect the physical horizon mechanism, and follow the evidence to exact source rows and frozen records. It separates complete physical episodes from reporting-clipped intervals and treats unknown or unsupported effects explicitly.

## Scientific-method case

The scientific story begins with a hypothesis that was not supported for the tested decision. R-01A retained dGR203’s scoped negative finding. R-01B investigated modeling choices and identified a separate large response at dGR101. R-01C then tested native NASA terrain products, common absolute observer elevation, uncensored episodes, numerical refinements and source support.

The supported conclusion is a reproducible terrain-representation response. Different product processing lineages are not treated as a formal asymptotic sequence, and no fine-grid duration plateau or independent physical truth is claimed. The unresolved historical replication discrepancy and the preferred numerical target missed in some spatial checks remain visible.

This is the basis for the intended **Best Use of Science** positioning: falsification, scoped controls, retained protocols, mechanistic explanation and explicit limits. It is a positioning argument, not an award prediction or a statement of current official judging rules.

## NASA-data case

RESOLVE uses Product 78 LOLA terrain, native Product 90 grids, the separate Product 104 SDEM, retained ancillary support data and the documented SPICE DE421 cartographic chain. Native products and the synthetic area-averaged control retain separate identities. Product provenance includes evaluated raster and kernel byte hashes, source paths and exact result downloads.

Acquired native-window hashes are labeled as such; they are not presented as unknown whole-remote-COG hashes. Support fields remain descriptive and do not calibrate correctness probability or measurement benefit.

This supports the intended **Best Use of Data** positioning: NASA datasets are used to interrogate the stability of a NASA-data-derived result, with provenance accessible to the reviewer.

## What a reviewer can do

- Explore dGR101, dGR201 and dGR204 within the retained November 2026 navigator interval.
- Inspect stored Sun/Earth positions relative to each supported nominal horizon and compare saved scenarios.
- Audit matched modeling-factor comparisons within their stated study scopes.
- Inspect dGR101’s paired skylines, observer elevations, event witnesses, terrain sections and reporting boundaries.
- Review the limited dGR203 negative evidence, retained protocols, numerical checks, exact source records and reproduction requirements.

The navigator’s reporting interval, the seasonal negative-control search and the complete-episode audit are separate scopes. Geometric visibility does not establish electrical power, a radio link budget, independent terrain accuracy or full mission feasibility.
