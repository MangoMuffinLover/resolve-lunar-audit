# Robustness Evaluation of South-Polar Observation and Lunar Visibility Estimates (RESOLVE)

uncoached judge comprehension check

Status: **not yet run**. Automated checks do not count as human comprehension evidence.

Use at least three people who have not received the RESOLVE pitch. Record their words, observations and misunderstandings. This small formative check is a product gate, not statistical validation.

## Twenty-second opening check

Start at the flagship audit in its initial state. Give only this instruction: “Explore this page for twenty seconds. Then tell me what the product does.” Do not explain terrain sensitivity or suggest a button.

After twenty seconds, hide the page and ask:

1. What problem does RESOLVE help someone investigate?
2. What did the first result mean, and what happened when it was audited?
3. What caused the different result?
4. Does RESOLVE establish which prediction is physically correct?

Record whether they identify the modeled result, the audit comparison, model sensitivity, and the horizon mechanism. Also record whether they mistake RESOLVE for a site-ranking tool, assume the coarser terrain is more accurate, or interpret a clipped interval as physical mission failure.

Proposed gate: every participant recognizes the audit purpose and avoids a terrain-truth or mission-certification claim; at least two of three can explain the result-to-horizon chain. Any repeated critical misunderstanding triggers a targeted copy or interaction fix before the final visual redesign. These are proposed acceptance criteria, not observed results.

## Evidence inspection check

After the opening check, allow two additional minutes on the science page. Ask:

1. Did the original dGR203 hypothesis receive support? What is the limit of that conclusion?
2. Are the approximately 13.23-day and 8.47-day results the same study question?
3. Did numerical refinement establish physical terrain accuracy?
4. Can you find the original result data and identify what would be needed to reproduce the research?

Record task success, time, first click, explanations and the exact point of confusion. Do not coach until the observation is finished. Keep any later assisted attempt separate from the first result.
